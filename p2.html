Zooniverse

College students struggled to use Zooniverse on mobile devices because the platform’s layout wasn’t sustainable on smaller screens. Many students had never heard of Zooniverse, and those who tried it found transcription tools difficult to use, especially when dealing with small text or staying organized. Interviews revealed that students needed clearer promotion, simpler tools, stronger motivation, and meaningful incentives to stay engaged.These insights reshaped our understanding of the platform by showing that usability and awareness were just as important as rewards. .

UX/UI Designer · Researcher · Prototype Developer Figma, Windows Notepad, & Manual Sketching

We ideated around real mobile use cases, imagining scenarios like a student transcribing during a noisy commute. Features such as audio playback, zoom‑in text, simple “yes/no” validation, highlighting tools, and save‑progress options were designed to make transcription easier anywhere. Students emphasized the importance of incentives, leading us to explore reward systems like money, scholarships, or service hours. Testing included task time, success scores, and usability surveys. Participants completed tasks with little to no confusion, and mobile users performed slightly faster than desktop users. Feedback confirmed strong usability, clear navigation, and effective accessibility features.

Our Figma prototype emphasized simplicity, consistency, and accessibility. Users could browse research categories, select projects, highlight text, listen to audio, save progress, and validate AI‑generated transcriptions using streamlined “yes/no” buttons. The interface used familiar layouts and Zooniverse’s existing color palette to maintain brand consistency. Testing showed 100% task success across all participants, with fast completion times and positive feedback on clarity and ease of use. If redesigning again, we would focus less on incentives and more on improving core transcription features to better support mobile users.
